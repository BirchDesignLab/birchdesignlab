A working handloom. Real drafts drive it: threading, tie-up, and
treadling, the same notation weavers have used for centuries. The
pattern is not painted on; it falls out of the system, the way it does
on wood and string.

Five drafts sit ready: plain weave, 2/2 twill, herringbone,
houndstooth, and goose eye. Every cell of the draft is editable if you
want to leave the map. The yarn shelf holds fourteen natural-dye
colors, and warp and weft each take a repeating stripe order, which is
where plaids come from. Weave pick by pick with the treadles, or let
the loom run. When the cloth pleases you, cut it free: the download is
a seamless tile, sized for a wallpaper.

Each day the loom opens already warped with a new pattern and
colorway. For readers who prefer the plate: a draft is a three-part
recipe. Threading says which of four shafts carries each warp thread.
The tie-up says which shafts each treadle lifts. Treadling says which
treadle each row uses. Lifted threads show; lowered threads hide. That
is the whole machine, and it has been enough for eight hundred years
of cloth.

The five drafts, written out. Threading and treadling repeat forever;
the tie-up lists what each treadle lifts.

```text
Plain weave
threading  1 2 3 4
tie-up     treadle 1 lifts shafts 1 3 · treadle 2 lifts shafts 2 4
treadling  1 2

2/2 twill
threading  1 2 3 4
tie-up     t1: 1 2 · t2: 2 3 · t3: 3 4 · t4: 4 1
treadling  1 2 3 4

Herringbone
threading  1 2 3 4 1 2 3 4 4 3 2 1 4 3 2 1
tie-up     t1: 1 2 · t2: 2 3 · t3: 3 4 · t4: 4 1
treadling  1 2 3 4

Houndstooth
threading  1 2 3 4
tie-up     t1: 1 2 · t2: 2 3 · t3: 3 4 · t4: 4 1
treadling  1 2 3 4
yarns      4 dark, 4 light, in both warp and weft

Goose eye
threading  1 2 3 4 3 2
tie-up     t1: 1 2 · t2: 2 3 · t3: 3 4 · t4: 4 1
treadling  1 2 3 4 3 2
```

The shelf, for the record: undyed ecru, undyed grey, walnut, oak
gall, iron black, madder, cochineal, onion skin, weld, flax, nettle,
heather, woad, and indigo. Fourteen colors, all of them dyes a
medieval weaver could have mixed.
