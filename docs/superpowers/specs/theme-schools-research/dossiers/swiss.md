# Swiss: school dossier

Reference for the Tier 3 revision of `/t/swiss/`. Compiled 09-23-26 from two researcher reports, then re-checked against the sources: every citation below is a page or scan I opened for this dossier. Where the reports disagreed, or a claim rested on a page I could not reopen, the claim is corrected, flagged, or dropped. Section 9 lists what was dropped and why.

Method note: museum records were read in a browser, so for the MoMA posters I looked at the object image as well as the record. The visual descriptions in sections 2 and 3 come from those images at screen size, not from secondary descriptions. *Grid Systems in Graphic Design* was read from the Internet Archive's OCR text [10]; OCR errors are silently corrected in paraphrase, and the one quotation was checked against the surrounding text. The Neue Grafik cover and spread [11] were rendered from the scan and looked at directly.

Source labels: **P** primary, **S** secondary, **W** weak (used only where stated, never relied on alone).

## 1. The school in one paragraph

The Swiss school (Zurich and Basel, roughly 1950 to 1965) held that visual communication should be built objectively: a grid of fields, one grotesque type family, flush-left asymmetric setting, and photography or constructed geometry instead of illustration. Its originators treated this as an ethic, not a look. Müller-Brockmann wrote that working with a grid expresses a constructive, forward-looking attitude and a professional ethos with the clarity of mathematical thinking, and he listed among its aims the will to bring surface and space under architectural control [10]. But the same book insists the grid is only an instrument: it makes interesting, contrasting and dynamic arrangements possible and is in itself no guarantee of success [10]; its purpose is material that is readily intelligible and structured with "a high degree of tension" [10]. Gerstner put the balance as the maximum of rule with the maximum of freedom [13]. The school published itself internationally from the start: *Neue Grafik*, edited by Lohse, Müller-Brockmann, Neuburg and Vivarelli, ran 18 issues from 1958 to 1965 in German, English and French [11][20]. An originator would recognise: one huge idea per sheet, type at extreme scale contrast, the grid felt in every alignment but never drawn, black or coloured fields, and the photograph as a working part of the composition.

## 3. Defining tells (critic's checklist)

Apply per page. Each item names what to look for and where it comes from.

1. **One idea at poster scale, everything else small.** Each exemplar has a single dominant element (a word, a photograph, a geometric figure) and small informational type [1][3][5][6][8][11]. Count the display-size elements on the page: the canon has one, occasionally two.
2. **Few type sizes.** Müller-Brockmann's worked examples use two or three: titles, text and captions in three sizes for *Casabella*, two sizes for *Oppositions*, a 7, 10 and 20 pt set for a grid demonstration [10]. The Neue Grafik cover uses essentially two [11]. Contrast comes from the jump between sizes, not from many intermediate ones.
3. **A grid of fields, not just columns.** The grid divides the page into fields separated by intervals of one or more text lines, and pictures take 1, 2, 3 or 4 fields [10]. Vertical positions are as governed as horizontal ones.
4. **The grid is felt, not drawn.** None of the posters [1] to [9] or the Neue Grafik cover [11] draws its grid. The structure shows as shared edges, hanging lines and consistent intervals. Gerstner notes that his grid looks complicated to anyone without the key [13]: it was a tool for the designer, not a pattern for the viewer.
5. **Asymmetry and unequal intervals.** Equal margins always read as indecision and dullness; margins of different sizes bring tension [10]. Nothing centred in the canon above.
6. **Tension is the goal.** Grid material should be structured with a high degree of tension [10]; pictures must dominate and activate the white space or the page looks tedious and dull [10]. A tidy page with no tension fails the school's own test.
7. **Orientation is available.** Words run vertically at full sheet height [5][8]; whole compositions rotate 45 degrees [2]; shapes fly on diagonals [1]. Rotation is used for the big element, not for small labels.
8. **Photography is structural.** Full-bleed or field-sized photographs, often duotone or tinted in the one colour [4][5][6][7]; *Grid Systems* gives a chapter to fitting photographs to the grid and to printing a colour area over a photograph so it dominates [10].
9. **Colour as a field.** Black sheets [3][5][6][8], a red photograph [4], a grey-violet or grey ground [1][2]. Colour covers the sheet or a whole grid field; it is rarely a small accent.
10. **One grotesque family, weight and size as the only variables.** *Grid Systems* discusses hierarchy through medium, semi-bold and bold of one face, noting the clear grey values each produces [10]. The Neue Grafik cover is one grotesque throughout [11].
11. **Lowercase is common.** "der Film" [3], "bauen in unserer zeit" [4], "auch Du bist liberal" [7], "allianz" [8], Bill's information block [9]. Not universal (the Neue Grafik title is capitalised [11]), but a strong period marker.
12. **Rhythm through programmed irregularity.** In the *musica viva* example in *Grid Systems*, the letters of "musica" are set at irregular intervals so that a rhythm is produced, with the programme lines aligned to those letters [10]. Rhythm comes from a rule applied unevenly on purpose, not from even repetition.
13. **Freedom inside the rule.** Gerstner: "the maximum of conformity to a rule with the maximum of freedom" [13]. A secondary account of the Tonhalle posters makes the same point about "the most complete freedom within the rigid system of the grid" [16].

## 4. Traps

- **The drawn grid as decoration.** Hairline column guides on screen are a design-tool overlay, not a period feature; the canon never shows its scaffolding (tell 4, [1] to [11]). Showing the grid is legitimate only as a teaching or construction moment (see section 6).
- **Tidy minimalism.** White space without a dominant element is exactly what *Grid Systems* calls tedious and dull [10]. "Clean" is not the school; tension is.
- **Everything big.** Many headline-size elements on one page flatten the hierarchy. The canon's scale contrast needs the small type to stay small (tells 1 and 2).
- **Equal rhythm.** Identical section after identical section (same rule, same index, same headline-left, text-right table) is the "equal margins" problem at page scale [10].
- **Accent-colour thinking.** One red used only for kickers, links and buttons reads as a UI accent. The canon uses colour as a field or a tinted photograph [4][7].
- **Monospace metadata and tech-brutalist furniture.** Mono index numbers and code-like captions belong to developer aesthetics, not to the school: the canon and its manual work within one grotesque family (tell 10). This is my reading of the evidence, not a claim any source makes.
- **Bauhaus overlap.** Max Bill is the bridge figure [9], and *Musica Viva* 1958 does use small red, yellow and blue squares [2], but in this portal the Bauhaus room owns primary-colour geometry. Swiss should lean on what Bauhaus does not: photography, rotated giant type, black fields, tinted photographs.
- **Faux print texture.** Some exemplars are linocut or letterpress [2][9], but grain overlays turn an objective school into a nostalgic one. No source supports adding texture; leave it out.

## 5. Palette and type evidence

**What the sources show.**
- Grounds: black is the most frequent field in the MoMA set [3][5][6][8]; also white paper [11], grey [2], grey-violet [1], buff [9].
- Colour: red appears as a tinted full-bleed photograph [4] and as small type on black [3]; orange-red duotone photography [7]; small primary squares [2]; blue-green-white for the *musica viva* poster described in *Grid Systems* [10].
- Type: a grotesque throughout. Akzidenz-Grotesk (Bauer and Berthold, first weight 1898) was already popular in postwar Western Europe, and Neue Haas-Grotesk's Swiss development reflected that popularity [18]. Neue Haas Grotesk was designed by Max Miedinger with art direction by Eduard Hoffmann for the Haas'sche Schriftgiesserei, later revised and renamed Helvetica by Linotype [19]. *Neue Grafik* was announcing Univers in 1959 [11].
- Setting: flush left, ragged right is the norm in *Grid Systems*' worked examples (the German captions specify ragged setting) [10]. The body of the Neue Grafik article appears justified in the scan [11], so ragged is the house default, not a law.

**What we have (package.json, @fontsource).** Archivo Variable (weight 100 to 900 and **width 62 to 125%**, via `standard.css`), Inter Variable (weight and optical size), Public Sans Variable, Space Grotesk, IBM Plex Mono, plus others that are wrong for this school.

**Mapping.**
- Keep Archivo as the single family. It is the only installed grotesque with a width axis, which gives the condensed cuts that tall rotated words need (tells 7 and 11, [5][8]). The route currently imports only `@fontsource-variable/archivo/wght.css` (`src/pages/t/swiss/[...page].astro` line 7); switching to `standard.css` unlocks `font-stretch: 62% 125%` at no licensing cost.
- Retire Public Sans and IBM Plex Mono for this school (tell 10). If Archivo body text proves too characterful at 17px, Inter is the closest installed neutral grotesque; that is a judgement call for the reviser, not a sourced claim.
- Colour: keep `--red: #da0016`, but add a black field state that is more than the dark scheme (black sheets are native in both schemes, [3][5][6][8]), and a red duotone treatment for photographs [4][7].

## 6. Motion vocabulary

Swiss posters are static; nothing below is period animation. These are motion ideas grounded in documented Swiss objects and in the school's written principles, for the transitions phase.

**Arrival and page-to-page.**
1. **Station-clock timing.** The Swiss station clock (Hans Hilfiker, designed 1944, introduced 1947) sweeps its red seconds hand continuously, reaches twelve one or two seconds early, and waits for the once-a-minute signal before the next minute begins [21]. Mondaine, the licensed maker, describes a roughly 58-second sweep followed by the pause at twelve [22]. As a transition signature: constant-velocity (linear) travel for most of the duration, a held beat, then a hard switch to the new state. No ease-out, no overshoot. Express it with a `linear` timing function plus a final hold (or a `linear()` curve with a flat tail). The 58:2 ratio is the source; compress it to taste inside the README's roughly 700 ms budget.
2. **The grid re-divides (Gerstner's mobile grid).** One measure divides into 1 to 6 columns without remainder [13]. Give each page its own division (for example Home in 2 fields, Services in 4, About in 3) and let the arrival animate the change of division: guides appear, slide to the new column lines, fields fill, guides withdraw. This is the one moment the grid may be drawn (trap 1), and it makes the grid a programme rather than wallpaper.
3. **Paste-up in field order.** Fields fill in a programmed, uneven order (tell 12, [10]), with hard edges. The current six-step column wipe (`theme.css`, `sw-wipe`) is a start; it is uniform and 240 ms, which reads as a flicker rather than a statement.
4. **Rotation into place.** A giant word arrives horizontal and turns 90 degrees into its vertical spine position [5][8], or a figure turns to 45 degrees [2]; constant velocity, hard stop.
5. **Wordmark handoff.** The founder likes the wordmark relocating while the page changes. In Swiss terms the billboard *is* the wordmark at poster scale (Home hero); the strongest move is the giant name collapsing into the header wordmark. The README allows one `transition:name` per school, so this needs an orchestrator decision.
6. **From another school.** `html[data-from-theme='y']` exists for entrances that depend on origin. A Swiss arrival from any school could strip colour first (to black and paper), then lay the grid: "objectivity" as the entrance.

**Within the page.**
- **Type as the moving surface.** Ruder's manual has chapters titled Rhythm, Variations and Kinetics [15]; I could not read their content, so treat this as a lead only. Archivo's width and weight axes make scale, weight and width change animatable without swapping fonts.
- **Blur belongs to the photograph.** Hofmann's *Giselle* carries motion as photographic blur while the type stays sharp [5]. If any in-page motion blurs, it is the image, never the type.
- **One moving thing per view**, following tell 1. Reduced motion: a hard cut, which is itself a Swiss answer.

## 7. Gap analysis against our build

Read from `src/themes/swiss/` and the renders in `scripts/themes/.out/swiss-f3/` (desktop) and `scripts/themes/.out/mobile-review/sheets/swiss.jpg` (mobile).

**What already lands.**
- The Home hero (`pages/Home.astro`, `.billboard`) is genuinely close to the Neue Grafik cover [11]: a stacked grotesque name flush left at huge size. About's "The shining tree" and Contact's "Start a conversation" have the same force. Scale contrast in the heroes is real (tell 1).
- Flush-left, ragged, asymmetric placement everywhere; nothing centred (tell 5).
- Services' four huge process numerals (`pages/Services.astro`, `.num`) echo the Neue Grafik "2" [11] and are the most Swiss thing below the fold.
- Dark as a negative print is well founded: black sheets are common in the canon [3][5][6][8]. Contact in dark is one of the strongest screens.
- No ornament, no shadows, no rounded corners; header and footer are quiet and correct.

**What is missing or wrong, and why it reads as lackluster.**
1. **The grid is drawn instead of used.** `Header.astro` renders `.sw-guides` and `theme.css` draws hairlines down the whole sheet; the code comment calls the grid "the picture". The canon never draws its grid (tell 4, trap 1). On screen the guides read as a Figma overlay, and they make every page look like a wireframe of a Swiss page rather than one.
2. **Columns only, no fields, no line-based rhythm.** `--cols` sets 4/6/12 columns; vertical space is multiples of `--u: 8px`, while body text sits on a 17px x 1.45 line (about 24.7px). *Grid Systems* measures field intervals in text lines (tell 3, [10]). The vertical rhythm is therefore arbitrary, and nothing aligns across rows, which is where grid tension comes from.
3. **Too many sizes, too many big things.** I count roughly fifteen distinct type sizes across `theme.css` and the pages (0.75, 0.8125, 0.875, 0.9375, 1, 1.0625, 1.125, 1.375, lead clamp, 2.75 to 7rem door and offering titles, 2.25 to 6.75rem pulls, 11rem numerals, 12rem closer, 16rem About title, 17.5rem billboard). Home alone has four display-size elements (billboard, door titles, lab pull, closer). The canon uses two or three sizes and one giant (tells 1 and 2, [10][11]). Below each hero the page settles into medium-large headings against body text, which is exactly the flat middle register the school avoids.
4. **One template, repeated.** Every page runs: mono index, giant headline, rule, index, headline-left and text-right table, red or ink band, footer. Hofmann's series and Gerstner's programme vary the composition under one rule set [6][7][13]. Our pages share a rule set and also share a layout, so the second page feels like the first.
5. **No rotation or diagonal of consequence.** The only rotated elements are the small red kickers (`.sw-vert`). The canon rotates the biggest element, at full sheet height [5][8], or the whole composition [2]. We have the gesture at the wrong scale.
6. **No photography.** The build forbids images; `meta.ts` lists icons and ornament in `forbids` and the pages carry none. Four of the nine canon posters are built on photographs [4][5][6][7], and *Grid Systems* devotes a chapter to them [10]. The repo has usable originals: `src/experiments/bdl-007/still.png` (the Shining Tree render) and the lab hero screenshots in `src/content/lab/*/hero.png`. A red duotone of one of them as a full-bleed field is period-correct [4][7]. Adding imagery is a founder call (see section 8).
7. **Red as accent, and fills as cards.** Red is spent on kickers, nav state, links, buttons, the etymology highlight and a hero rectangle. The hero's red block (`.block`) holds a line of copy but does not stand for anything; the canon's geometry stands for its subject [1][2]. The ink and red bands use `.sw-bleed`, which on desktop only extends by the gutter (`theme.css`, `margin-inline: calc(-1 * var(--gutter))`), so they read as inset cards (visible in the Home and About desktop renders) rather than fields. The canon's fields cover the sheet [3][4][5][6][8].
8. **Three families.** Archivo, Public Sans and IBM Plex Mono (`meta.ts` fonts). The canon works in one grotesque (tell 10). The mono section indices and the mono colophon line are the most off-period detail on the page.
9. **Arrival is too slight.** `sw-wipe` is 240 ms in six uniform steps, and the wordmark group moves in 240 ms. At that speed and evenness it does not register as a programme (section 6, ideas 1 to 3).

The diagnosis in one sentence: our Swiss room has the rules of the school and very little of the tension the rules were for. The grid is shown rather than exploited, the type scale has many steps instead of one violent jump, colour is an accent rather than a field, and there is no photograph or rotated giant to give the page an image.

## 8. Priorities for Tier 3

Most important first.

1. **One giant per page, two or three sizes for everything else.** Collapse the type scale in `theme.css` and the pages to text, lead and one display size (plus the Services numerals as the page's giant if Services keeps them). Demote door titles, offering titles and pulls to the lead size. Tells 1 and 2 [10][11].
2. **Give one element per page a rotation.** Run a condensed Archivo word vertically at full viewport height (Giselle and Allianz [5][8]), or turn a figure to 45 degrees (Musica Viva [2]). Candidates: About's "tree", Services' "properly.", or an `aria-hidden` giant on Home. Replace the small `.sw-vert` kickers rather than adding to them. Tell 7.
3. **Stop drawing the grid; build fields instead.** Remove the always-on `.sw-guides`, set the vertical unit to the body line (for example `--u` equal to the body line-height) and define rows as well as columns so blocks, captions and baselines align across the sheet. Keep the guides for the arrival only (section 6, idea 2). Tells 3 and 4 [10][13].
4. **Make colour a field.** Full-bleed black and red fields to the sheet edge, not gutter-bleed cards; allow a whole hero to be a black sheet in the light scheme ([3][5][6][8]). Retire red as a link and highlight colour where it competes. Tell 9.
5. **Add one photograph as structure (founder decision).** A red or black duotone of an existing studio image (BDL-007 still, a lab hero) as a full-bleed field on Home or About, with type set over or beside it on the grid ([4][7], *Grid Systems* on colour areas over photographs [10]). If the founder declines imagery, use a constructed figure that stands for the content instead, as the Tonhalle posters do [1].
6. **One family.** Switch the route import to `@fontsource-variable/archivo/standard.css`, drop Public Sans and Plex Mono, and use Archivo width and weight for all hierarchy, including section numbers. Tells 10 and 11.
7. **Vary the composition per page under one programme.** Assign each page its own column division and hero structure (Gerstner's mobile grid [13], Hofmann's series [6]) so Home, About and Services stop sharing a layout. Consider lowercase for the billboard and giants via CSS only (tell 11; check parity is unaffected).
8. **Arrival as a programme.** Station-clock timing (linear sweep, hold, hard switch [21][22]) applied to a grid re-division and field paste-up, 500 to 700 ms, with a hard cut under reduced motion. Raise the wordmark handoff (one `transition:name` limit) with the orchestrator.

## 9. Dropped, corrected and unverified

- **"Compositions angled about 45 degrees" (founding-texts report, attributed to SOCKS [16]).** Not in the source. The article does not mention angles. Corrected: a 45-degree rotation is visible in *Musica Viva* 1958 [2], and diagonals in the 1953 Tonhalle poster [1], from the images themselves.
- **"The grid system is an aid, not a guarantee."** Not found in the OCR text of [10]. What the book says (in the section on examples with three fields) is that the grid gives the designer an instrument for interesting, contrasting and dynamic arrangements but is in itself no guarantee of success. Paraphrased above instead.
- **Ruder's "Wesentliches" essay series (1957 to 1959) and the quote "Everything is movement".** The Letterform Archive article [17] does not contain either; dropped. The content of Ruder's Kinetics chapter is also unverified; only chapter titles are used [15].
- ***Der Film* as a landscape poster.** MoMA's record gives 90.1 x 127.6 cm, but its image displays as portrait [3]. Orientation unresolved; not used.
- **Lohse's *Allianz* as "modular colour fields".** The image shows giant rotated lowercase type on black [8]. Corrected.
- ***Weniger Lärm* 1960 (MoMA 513.1983).** Record opened (offset lithograph, 127.7 x 90.1 cm, printer Lithographie & Cartonnage AG, acquired by exchange) but MoMA shows no image, and the Swiss Automobile Club commission was not verified. Left out of the canon.
- **Helvetica details** (Graphic 57 in Lausanne, the 1960 renaming date): not verified against a primary foundry document. Only the designer, art director, foundry and renaming by Linotype are used [19].
- **Max Bill's 1946 essay "Über Typographie"**, **Hofmann's *Graphic Design Manual* (1965)**, **the Beethoven poster (1955)** and **the SBB design manual (1980)**: not opened in primary form. The Museum für Gestaltung eMuseum is behind a bot check, which I did not attempt to pass. The only online SBB manual record found is a student-contributed archive listing, too weak to cite. These are the next things to verify if Tier 3 needs them.
- **Letterform Archive dating.** [17] was read through a text tool that reported a 1957 date for Ruder's *Typographie*; the bookseller listing of the first edition gives 1967 [15], which is the date used here.
