# Vaporwave: school dossier

Reference for the Tier 3 revision of `/t/vaporwave/`. Compiled 09-23-26 from two researcher reports, then re-checked against the sources: every citation below is a page I opened for this dossier. Where the two reports disagreed, or a claim rested on a page I could not reopen, the claim is corrected, flagged, or dropped. Section 9 lists what was dropped and why.

Method note: pages were read through a fetch tool that returns a text rendering. It cannot see images, so no claim here describes the look of an album cover from the image itself. Revisers should look at the covers directly on the Bandcamp pages cited ([1], [5], [6], [7]) before making palette or layout calls from them.

Source labels: **P** primary, **S** secondary, **W** weak (used only where stated, never relied on alone).

---

## 1. The school in one paragraph

Vaporwave is an internet music scene of roughly 2010 to 2013 whose records were built from the audio and imagery of corporate life: promotional sounds, shopping-mall and lobby atmospheres, software, and the "plaza" of consumer space. Adam Harper, writing as the scene formed, described it as music that adopts "(near-) contemporary sounds and imagery from corporate promotionals" [4], and pictured its world as senses riding escalators through spotless artificial environments [4]. The records name themselves after software and booting machines (Floral Shoppe opens on a track called "ブート", boot [1]; Blank Banshee 0 runs from "B:/Start Up" to "B:/Shut Down/Depression" [5]), title themselves in Japanese [1][7], and invent corporations and leisure facilities (PrismCorp's "SuperMall", "Wellness Center", "Neo Spa & Salon" [6]). Its founding scholar reads it as working over Muzak and commercial music to undercut the sale of nostalgia [8]. An originator would recognise: corporate leisure, obsolete consumer software, Japanese titling, classical sculpture treated as stock imagery, and loops. They would treat the neon perspective grid and the car at night as a neighbour's language (section 4).

## 3. Defining tells (critic's checklist)

Apply to each page. A page does not need every tell; it needs no false ones.

1. **Corporate leisure space.** Malls, lobbies, spas, cinemas, resorts, information desks: the privatised public space of the "virtual plaza" [4][6][1 (情報デスクVIRTUAL)]. Is there a *place* on the page, and is it a commercial one?
2. **Obsolete consumer software as the frame.** Boot, start-up and shut-down; file paths; program and file names as titles [1][5][6]. Does the chrome name a real OS convention (title bar buttons, taskbar buttons, bevelled borders [11][14]) rather than a generic "old computer"?
3. **Japanese as titling.** Titles and labels in Japanese, sometimes mixed with Latin in one line ("外ギン Aviation", "ECCOと悪寒ダイビング" [1]; 新しい日の誕生 [7]). Is the Japanese a *title for something*, not scattered texture?
4. **Classical sculpture as stock image.** A real, damaged museum object lifted out of context [9], not a reverent neoclassical setting. Does the antiquity read as found and cut out, or as architecture built for the page?
5. **The loop.** Short excerpts repeated with echo and pitch shift [3, W]; covers that move as loops [7]. Does the motion repeat, audibly or visibly, rather than progress?
6. **Corporate promotional register.** The tone of a promo video or a welcome screen [4]. Do the decorative labels (window titles, OSD, badges) sound like a company addressing a customer?
7. **The system as the event.** Boot and shutdown are the bookends of two founding records [1][5]. Are arrivals and departures staged as the machine starting and stopping?

## 4. Traps

1. **Outrun / synthwave / retrowave read as vaporwave.** The neighbour's primary material is the car at night: Kavinsky's *OutRun* (Record Makers, 02-22-13) takes its name from the Sega arcade game and carries tracks "Testarossa Autodrive", "Nightcall", "Roadgame" and "DeadCruiser", with a bio that begins in 1986 [15]. None of the vaporwave release pages I opened ([1], [2], [5], [6], [7]) uses road, car or grid language; they use malls, software and Japanese titles. The neon perspective grid, the chrome 1980s logo and the striped sun over low-poly mountains belong to the synthwave side by convention. Honest limit: I found no primary source that documents the *origin* of the grid-and-striped-sun image itself; the researchers' scholarly support for the split (a 2021 quote from Paul Ballam-Cross, via Lucas Cantinelli in *Imaginaires*) is a paper I could not reopen. Treat the attribution as well-founded convention, secondary-sourced. The two scenes do blend at the edges: 2814's "neon utopia" [7] is vaporwave by label and lineage.
2. **Sunset and palms: framing decides the family.** Sunsets and palms are not foreign to vaporwave: PrismCorp's catalogue has "Neo Sunsetters", "Palisades", "Sol Air" [6]. As *resort and timeshare* imagery (a brochure, a lobby mural, a screensaver in a travel agency) they are vaporwave. As the view over a car bonnet at dusk, speeding down a grid, they are outrun [15].
3. **Generic retro computer.** An unbranded CRT with no specific OS grammar reads as stock. The real grammar is documented: two-colour title bar gradients (left active caption colour, right gradient colour), separate active and inactive caption colours, 3D highlight and shadow edges on controls [14]; title bar icon and buttons, taskbar buttons [11]; clock in the taskbar [12].
4. **Katakana as wallpaper.** The canon titles things in Japanese [1][7]; random kana sprinkled as texture is the meme, not the records.
5. **Neoclassical instead of vaporwave.** Clean, complete, lovingly drawn columns and friezes read as a Greek revival. The canon's object is broken (a head missing its crown [9]) and presented as a cut-out photograph. Gold would tip it fully into neoclassical (already forbidden in `meta.ts`).
6. **A palette presented as canon.** The quintet in `theme.css` (#ff71ce, #01cdfe, #05ffa1, #b967ff, #fffb96) is a community-circulated "vaporwave palette". I found no canon source for it. It can stay as the build's palette; it should not be cited as the genre's.
7. **Unsourced lore.** The coinage of the word "vaporwave", the "vaporware" pun, and fullwidth "ＡＥＳＴＨＥＴＩＣ" lettering are all widely repeated; none was verified against a primary source here. Do not put them in wall labels as fact.

## 5. Palette and type evidence

**Palette.** The text sources give register, not hex: corporate promotional imagery [4], leisure and resort names [6], a "neon utopia" [7]. Palette claims must come from looking at the covers ([1], [5], [6], [7]); I could not. What the build has:

- Dark (native): night sky #080119 to #2c0b58, horizon #ff4fa8, cyan grid #01cdfe, neon quintet as accents. This is the synthwave night palette (trap 1).
- Light, "pastel dawn": peach #ffe4d6 to lavender #ebe0ff, magenta grid #c01f86, ink #2a0a4a. Both researchers characterised vaporwave's own palette as pastel (pink, teal, lavender) against synthwave's saturated neon on black; that characterisation is secondary and unverified by me, but if the founder wants the school to read as more precisely vaporwave, the light scheme is already the nearer one. Nothing here argues for changing `nativeScheme`.
- Windows grammar colours the build could borrow directly: the active/inactive caption pairs and the 3D highlight/shadow edge roles [14]. The build already models the edge roles (`--bevel-hi`, `--bevel-lo`); it has no inactive caption state.

**Type.** Installed and usable (package.json, @fontsource): VT323, Libre Caslon Display, Dela Gothic One, Exo 2, IBM Plex Mono, plus the serif and grotesk families listed there. Findings:

- **Kana are not in a webfont.** Every kana element (`.vw-kana`, `.tray-kana`, `.opener-kana`, `.intro-kana`) is set in an OS fallback stack ('Yu Gothic', 'Hiragino Kaku Gothic ProN', 'Noto Sans JP', 'Meiryo'), so glyph shape varies by device. `@fontsource/dela-gothic-one` is installed with its Japanese subsets (`japanese-400.css`, unicode-range sliced into numbered files, so only the slices a page uses download), but `src/pages/t/vaporwave/[...page].astro` imports only `latin-400.css`. Dela Gothic One is a Japanese gothic display face; it is already the billboard face. Setting the kana in it unifies the Japanese with the "BIRCH" chrome word and makes it identical on every device. Weight: check the transfer cost of the slices actually hit (the kana strings are short).
- **VT323** (terminal face) fits the OSD, prompts and title bars: the software tell [5].
- **Libre Caslon Display** carries headings. No canon source ties a didone or Caslon display to vaporwave; it reads as the "luxury brand in a mall" register, which fits tell 6. Keep; not evidenced either way.
- The chrome gradient on "BIRCH" (`--bb-chrome`, hard horizon line through the letters) is the 1980s chrome-logo idiom: synthwave side of trap 1 by convention.

## 6. Motion vocabulary

For the later transitions phase. Ordered from best sourced to most inferred.

1. **Boot and shutdown as arrival and departure.** Two founding records open on a boot and close on a shutdown ("ブート" [1]; "B:/Start Up" to "B:/Shut Down/Depression" [5]). Arriving at vaporwave from another school = the machine starting (splash, progress, desktop paints in, taskbar last). Leaving = shutting down. This is the most distinctive, best-sourced per-school transition available, and it extends naturally to the founder's idea of distinct transitions between particular pairs of schools (the other school's page as what was on screen before the boot).
2. **Window verbs, period-correct.** Microsoft's shell animation API offers exactly four: roll, slide, collapse or expand (from or to the centre), and alpha fade, with left/right/up/down direction flags combinable for diagonals [13]. Typical duration: 200 milliseconds [13]. Anything else (3D flips, springy overshoot) is outside the grammar. Caveat: the API page's minimum client is Windows 2000 [13]; the 1995 guidelines have an "Animation" section [11] a reviser could read via Internet Archive lending [10] to confirm the Windows 95 set.
3. **Minimise to the taskbar, restore from it.** Within the school, a page change can collapse the current page's windows toward their taskbar button and expand the new page's windows from the newly pressed button. The build already names the wordmark as a view-transition element; the task buttons and `.vw-win` elements are the next candidates. This is the founder's "wordmark transforms while the page changes under it" idea, in OS grammar.
4. **Take the shadow off while moving.** Microsoft's own note: avoid animating a window that has a drop shadow, because it looks distracting and jerky [13]. The build's windows carry a hard 12px offset shadow (`.vw-win`, `box-shadow: 12px 12px 0 var(--win-drop)`); drop it for the duration of any window animation, restore it on landing. Cheap, period-true, and fixes a real rendering risk.
5. **The loop, with its seam showing.** The music's unit is a short excerpt repeated with echo and pitch shift [3, W]; covers moved as loops [7]. Within-page motion can be a loop that visibly restarts, short and repeating, rather than an endless smooth scroll. Design inference; the sonic description rests on a weak source. The researchers' stronger text for this (Tanner on audible loop seams, and a chopped-and-screwed tempo lineage) was not verifiable here (section 9).
6. **Inactive to active caption.** Focus moves by the title bar changing from inactive to active colours [14]. On scroll or hover, the window in view becomes active and the others go inactive: quiet, correct, and legible as "an OS".
7. **Existing moves to keep.** The CRT power-on and VHS tracking wobble (`theme.css`, arrival block) are recorded-media hardware, not contradicted by any source; keep them as the school's texture while the boot and window verbs take the structural role. All of it stays under the reduced-motion guard already in place.

## 7. Gap analysis against the build

### What lands (protect it)

- **Taskbar header** (`Header.astro`): wordmark as Start button, nav as task buttons with the current page pressed, tray with a clock at the right. Matches the taskbar grammar [11] and the clock placement [12]. Keep.
- **Windows** (`parts/Win.astro`, `.vw-win*` in `theme.css`): title bar icon, three caption buttons, underlined menu accelerators, address bar, bevel edges with light top-left and dark bottom-right. Matches documented chrome [11][14]. Keep.
- **File-name titles and prompts**: `software.exe`, `web_design.htm`, `custom_software.exe`, `setup.exe`, `new_message.eml`, `transmission.sys`, `founder.txt`, the `C:\>` kicker. The same move as "B:/Start Up" [5]. Among the most canon-accurate things in the build. Keep.
- **Japanese as titles** (`Home.astro` バーチ・デザイン・ラボ and デザイン, `About.astro` シャイニング・ツリー, `Services.astro` サービス, `Sent.astro` メッセージ送信完了, tray バーチ): each names something on the page, which is the canon's usage [1][7], not wallpaper. Keep.
- **Classical material** (`parts/Column.astro`, `Marble.astro`, `Meander.astro`, `Rune.astro`, About's temple and plinth): the right family [9]. Keep, with the note in the gaps below.
- **Services as a desktop, contact as a mail client, sent as a system dialog**: the software frame [5][6] carried through every page. Keep.
- **Recorded-media OSD** (`Home.astro` "PLAY", "SP 0:19:85", tray clock stuck at 12:00): coherent texture; not contradicted by any source.
- **Light scheme**: the nearer of the two schemes to the pastel characterisation (section 5).

### What is missing or off

1. **No plaza.** The canon's centre, corporate leisure space [4][6], is absent. The build has an OS and a temple, but nowhere a mall, lobby, atrium, spa or information desk. The footer checkerboard (`Footer.astro`, `.floor`) is the only hint, and it currently reads as an outrun floor. Vektroid literally recorded as 情報デスクVIRTUAL [1]: the Lab CRT (`Home.astro`, `.crt`) could become an information-desk kiosk; the checkerboard could become lobby tile.
2. **The hero is the neighbour's picture** (`fx.ts`, `Horizon.astro`, `Palm.astro`, `.vw-floor` in `theme.css`, `--bb-chrome` billboard). Grid scrolling toward the viewer, chrome logo, sun behind low-poly mountains: synthwave by convention (trap 1). The founder rates it highly; this is a framing decision, not a correction. Options: (a) keep and own it as a deliberate synthwave inflection, naming it so in `meta.ts`; (b) keep the sun and palms but reframe as resort imagery [6] (a lobby mural or travel-agency screensaver inside an OS window or frame, slower, looping); (c) move the grid to a supporting role and give the hero to the plaza or a bust.
3. **`meta.ts` copy leans synthwave.** `era` says "dreaming of 1985" and `signature` leads with "a neon grid running to the horizon". The canon's dream, per its own titles, is corporate software and leisure [1][5][6]. If option (a) above, keep; otherwise rewrite `signature` around software, plaza and statuary.
4. **Antiquity is architecture, not a found object.** Columns and friezes are pristine vector drawings (`Column.astro`, `Meander.astro`). The canon's object is a damaged head cut out of a museum photograph [9] (trap 5). There is no bust or head anywhere in the build. The actual Helios photograph is CC0 [9], so it is legally usable; using it would change `meta.ts` `assets.provenance` from `original-vector`, which is a founder decision.
5. **Motion is metronomic.** The grid scroll (`.vw-floor::before`, `1.6s linear infinite`; `fx.ts` `uScroll` at `time * 0.55`) is a steady forward drive, which is the outrun sensation. The canon's motion is the loop [3, W][7]. See section 6, item 5.
6. **No inactive windows.** Every title bar uses the same active gradient (`.vw-win-bar`). Real chrome distinguishes active and inactive captions [14]; Services shows two overlapping windows that should read as focused and unfocused.
7. **Title bar gradient is three-stop** (pink, lavender, cyan). The documented gradient is two colours, left to right [14]. Minor; a stylisation, acceptable if deliberate.
8. **Kana depend on OS fonts** while Dela Gothic One's Japanese subsets sit unused (section 5).
9. **Window shadows would jerk under animation** once transitions arrive [13] (section 6, item 4).
10. **Mixed-script titling is unused.** The canon mixes Japanese and Latin in one title [1]; the build keeps them in separate elements. Window titles or decorative labels (aria-hidden only) could mix, e.g. a title bar reading in both scripts.

## 8. Priorities for Tier 3

Most important first. Items 2 and 4 carry founder decisions; bring them to him rather than resolving them.

1. **Build the plaza.** Add the corporate-leisure layer the canon is built on [4][6]: turn the Lab CRT into an information-desk kiosk (the 情報デスクVIRTUAL alias [1]), reframe the checkerboard as lobby tile, and let decorative labels speak in a welcome-to-the-facility register. Decoration only; copy is fixed.
2. **Decide the hero's family (founder call).** Present options (a) own the synthwave inflection, (b) reframe sun and palms as resort imagery [6], (c) demote the grid. Whatever he picks, align `meta.ts` `era` and `signature` with it.
3. **Stage arrival and departure as boot and shutdown** [1][5], with within-school page changes as windows collapsing to and expanding from their taskbar buttons, 200 ms per window, roll/slide/collapse/fade only [13]. This is the school's distinct transition for the transitions phase.
4. **Add a found classical head (founder call on provenance).** Either the CC0 Helios photograph [9] cut out and treated as stock, or a drawn bust that keeps `original-vector`. Keep the columns.
5. **Active and inactive title bars** [14]: one focused window per view, the rest inactive; on Services the back window goes inactive.
6. **Set the kana in Dela Gothic One** by importing its Japanese subset in the vaporwave route file; measure the added bytes.
7. **Make the grid a loop, not a drive**: a short cycle that visibly restarts, or a slower cadence, rather than `linear infinite` forward motion [3, W][7]. Reduced-motion still frame unchanged.
8. **Remove window shadows during window animations** [13].
9. **Wall label accuracy**: keep the placard off unsourced lore (trap 7) and off the palette-as-canon claim (trap 6).

## 9. What was dropped or corrected, and open gaps

Corrections to the researcher reports:

- The "straight out of a timeshare sales video presentation" line on the PrismCorp page is a **supporter's comment**, not the artist's liner text [6]. Not used as the artist's framing.
- The claim that the Eccojams cover is plain was unsupported; the community upload describes the cover as referencing *Ecco the Dolphin* [3, W]. Neither is used.
- The Floral Shoppe cover's Japanese text was reported as 花の専門店. That string is verified only as the title of **track 3** [1]; the cover lettering was not verified.
- Harper's article: the Dummy URL now returns 404 (dmy.co) and web.archive.org is unreachable from the fetch tool. His own blog post is cited instead [4]. The quotes the second researcher recovered from Harper via Cantinelli ("belongs in the plaza", "both and neither", "early 1990s onwards") and the "vaporware" pun could not be checked and are dropped.
- Tanner quotes reported via Cantinelli (skepticism of capitalism's promise; loop seams and the audible click; anonymity) are dropped; only the author's own summary [8] is used. The publisher page (collectiveinkbooks.com) returned 403.
- The *New Dreams Ltd.* album (artist Laserdisc Visions) is a 2020 fan upload to the Internet Archive [16, W]; it lists a track titled "Malls", consistent with tell 1, but carries no date authority.

Open gaps (for a follow-up with a raised search budget, or a browser):

- Lucas Cantinelli, "Anemoia and the Vaporwave Phenomenon", *Imaginaires* no. 26 (Université de Reims): the peer-reviewed source both the vaporwave/synthwave split and several quotes rest on. Needs its URL found and reopened.
- A primary record of James Ferraro, *Far Side Virtual* (Hippos in Tanks, 2011), and Ferraro's own statements (The Quietus interview was not opened).
- Chopped-and-screwed lineage (University of Houston news, DJ Mag feature): not opened; the tempo-stutter motion idea is unsourced until then.
- VHS scanline and tracking-noise descriptions: design-blog sourcing only.
- The text of the 1995 Windows guidelines, chapters 6, 10, 13 and 14 [10][11], especially its "Animation" section.
- Origin of the grid-and-striped-sun image (trap 1).
- Coinage of the word "vaporwave".
- The covers themselves, looked at by eye, for palette.
