# Cottagecore: research dossier

School id `cottagecore`, portal `/t/cottagecore/`. Written 09-23-26 for the Tier 3 revisers. Read with `src/themes/cottagecore/` open.

How to read this: every factual claim carries a bracketed number that points to the source list at the end. Each source there is marked PRIMARY, SECONDARY or WEAK, and I opened every one of them. Where I could not open a source, or could find only weak ones, the text says so. Sections 7 and 8 are my own judgement. They apply the sources to our build and are not sourced facts.

---

## 1. The school in one paragraph

Cottagecore is an internet aesthetic. It formed on Tumblr around 2017 to 2018, got its own subreddit in August 2018, and went mainstream on TikTok during the 2020 lockdowns [29][30]. It pictures a small, hand-made rural life: baking, gardening, pressing flowers, sewing, writing letters. Scholars read it as a modern pastoral, an imagined refuge from the city that is carefully built for the screen [30]. It knowingly inherits William Morris's Arts and Crafts argument against industrial manufacture, and it contradicts that argument by existing only through digital platforms [31]. Its visual sources are mostly Victorian: cottage watercolours such as Helen Allingham's, and rural fiction [32]. Its originators on Tumblr would recognise it by one feeling. The page is a table someone actually works at, not a showroom. Everything on it was picked, pressed, written or stitched by a hand, and the hand shows.

## 2. The canon

Each exemplar below has a real object record, and each shows something our build can learn from.

- **Emily Dickinson's herbarium, c. 1839 to 1846, Houghton Library, Harvard** [1]. A schoolgirl's pressed-plant album, made at Amherst Academy and labelled with common and Latin names. It is too fragile to handle, so the public now meets it as a digital facsimile. That is a precedent for showing pressed flowers as images rather than faking physical texture.
- **Henry Bradbury, *A Few Leaves from the Newly-Invented Process of "Nature-Printing"*, 1854, V&A 14765:19** [15]. Plants were moulded, inked and run through a press, and the prints look like "flattened herbarium specimens". It shows what pressing really does: things go flat, and edges stay precise.
- **RHS herbarium practice** [18]. This is the living method. The specimen is arranged while still pliable, pressed under weight, dried for two days to three weeks, fixed with gummed linen tape, and labelled bottom right with name, family, place, date and collector.
- **William Morris, *Strawberry Thief*, designed 1883, V&A T.586-1919** [2]. Indigo-discharged and block-printed cotton. It shows the thrushes that stole strawberries from Morris's own kitchen garden at Kelmscott. It was one of the firm's most expensive prints. The nature is observed at first hand, and the colour comes from the dye process.
- **Morris's lectures, 1877 to 1882** [19][20][21]. The theory behind the whole school. Art has to show "obvious traces of the hand of man" [20]. Ornament should grow out of its material [20]. Pattern should hide its repeat and grow "visibly and necessarily" from one part to the next [21].
- **Gingham, Mangaluru, c. 1855, V&A 5296(IS)** [3]. A blue and white check woven from dyed yarn. This is the object that gingham is named for, and its home is South India, not an English cottage. The V&A holds a whole series of these, dating from 1855 [3].
- **Laura Ashley dress, 1972 to 1975, V&A T.279-1988** [5], and **Gina Fratini gingham dress, 1970s, V&A T.113-1996** [4]. The 1970s commercial revival of sprigged cotton and gingham. This is cottagecore's direct pre-internet ancestor.
- **Beatrix Potter, unused *Peter Rabbit* watercolour, 1902, V&A LC 22/A/2** [7], and **unused *Johnny Town-mouse* watercolour, 1918, V&A BP.525** [8]. Both are small. The first is 209 x 300 mm and the second 90 x 110 mm, in watercolour and sepia ink inside a single ruled border. Both are close vignettes, not landscapes.
- **Potter's microscope studies of a ground beetle, 1887, and her 1890 toy picture with a lift-up flap** [9][10]. The rigour under the charm is real: formal art examinations and scientific drawing. The flap is a period reveal device.
- **Helen Allingham, *A Cottage at Chiddingfold*, 1889, V&A P.20-1917** [11], and ***A Cottage in the Isle of Wight*, c. 1880 to 1900, V&A P.8-1931** [12]. The Victorian cottage idyll the aesthetic draws on [32]. It is observed and specific: a named village, a woman with laundry.
- **Eliza Richardson's sampler, 1837, V&A T.3-1930** [14]. Cross-stitch in silk on wool, worked by a ten-year-old. The text is stitched, not printed, and it is signed with her name, her age and the year.
- **Manuscript cookbooks, Schlesinger Library, 1704 to 1994** [16], and **an English recipe book, mid-17th to early 19th century, Wellcome Collection** [17]. Handwritten, often in several hands, with recipes mixed in among remedies, accounts and pasted newspaper clippings. Entries were added over decades.
- **mt washi masking tape, Kamoi Kakoshi, launched 2007** [23]. A decorative line made from an industrial tape. The company's own words for how it is used: "cut & tear, layer up, write & stamp".

## 3. Defining tells: a checklist for critics

Apply each item to a page. The number says where the tell comes from.

1. **Specific, not generic.** A named plant, a dated entry, a labelled specimen. Canon nature is always observed and identified: Morris's thrushes, Dickinson's Latin labels, Potter's dated beetle [1][2][10][18]. A bare "flower" or a scribble where a label should be fails.
2. **More than one hand.** Real recipe books carry several handwritings, later additions and pasted clippings [16][17]. A single handwriting face in a single ink everywhere reads as a font, not a person.
3. **Pressed means flat.** Pressing is one-way: it ends flat, dry and fixed with tape [15][18]. Specimens should sit in the paper, not float above it on a shadow.
4. **Firm outline on a soft surface.** Morris: "Definite form bounded by firm outline is a necessity" [21]. The softness belongs to the colour and the paper, never to blurry drawing.
5. **Hide the repeat.** Morris wanted a pattern masked "enough to prevent people from counting the repeats" [21]. Any texture whose tile you can count (torn edges, tape, scallops) weakens the made-by-hand reading.
6. **Material logic.** Every surface should behave like its material. Gingham is two dyed yarns crossing, so the crossings darken [3]. Washi tape is translucent and can be torn [23]. Block print and dye set the colour [2]. Ornament grows from the material [20].
7. **Small, intimate scale.** Canon images are pocket-sized vignettes inside a ruled border [7][8]. The hero is a sheet on a table, not a poster.
8. **Rigour under the charm.** Potter drew through a microscope and sat art examinations [10]. Morris wrote argued lectures [19][20][21]. The page should be well made underneath, never naive or twee.
9. **Signed and dated.** Samplers carry a name, an age and a year [14]. Herbarium labels carry a date and a collector [18]. Made things say who made them and when. For us that must be the studio, never the founder (see 7.2, item 4).

## 4. Traps

- **Mass-market rustic.** The V&A holds the proof. An Allingham watercolour [11] and the 1989 Coalport "Country Cottages" plate made from her work, transfer-printed with a gilded rim [13], show the observed original turning into souvenir decoration. Mid-century "Patio Gingham" tableware was collected as a mass-produced popular design [6]. Barn wood, mason jars and chalkboard signs are the modern version of this. Test: can the motif be traced to something observed and made? If not, it is decor.
- **Laura Ashley pastiche.** The 1970s sprigged-cotton milkmaid look [5] is an ancestor, not the target. Overdo matching florals and it slides into that decade's commercial nostalgia, and into Grandmillennial, which the portal treats as a separate school. Cottagecore is sparse single objects on a table, not coordinated abundance.
- **Twee or kidcore.** Potter's subjects are small animals, but her record is scientific drawing and professional training [10]. No cartoon animals, no bubble lettering.
- **Tradwife drift.** Research on over 200,000 Tumblr posts found no explicit pipeline from cottagecore to tradwife content. It did find that tradwife posts under the cottagecore tag shifted after 2021 toward religion, traditional gender roles and homesteading [33]. The build uses none of that iconography and should keep it that way. Domestic here means craft, not a gender role.
- **Escapism without admitting it.** Scholars call the aesthetic "constantly in conflict with itself": a digital fantasy of a pre-digital life [31]. For a software studio this is useful, not embarrassing. The honest move is to let the page be plainly a web page doing handwork, not to pretend it is paper.
- **Stock-cozy photography.** Warm-filtered photos of teacups and knitted blankets have no maker, date or place. None of the canon is stock. (This is a judgement, not a sourced finding.)

## 5. Palette and type evidence

**What the sources show about colour.**
- Gingham in the canon is **blue and white**: the Mangaluru lengths [3] and the Fratini dress [4].
- Morris's signature colour is **indigo**, with alizarin red and weld yellow laid over the blue and white ground [2].
- Laura Ashley's revival used **navy with a white sprig** [5].
- Potter worked in watercolour with **sepia ink** [8].
- Allingham painted **red brick, blossom and green lawn** [11].
- Nature-printing was done in black and two greens [15].
- Taken together: dye colours (indigo, madder or alizarin red, weld yellow), ink colours (sepia, iron-black), and plant greens on cream paper.

**How our palette maps.** Linen `#f5eddd`, paper `#fffaf0`, ink-brown `#3a2a1f`, sage, rose-deep `#9b4652` and butter are all defensible. The ink-brown sits close to sepia ink [8], rose-deep to madder or alizarin red [2], and butter to weld yellow [2]. **Blue is the canon's missing colour.** Our gingham is sage, which is a valid choice, but the one gingham with a museum record is indigo and white [3][4]. A single indigo thread (a tape, one ink, one specimen label) would tie the build to Morris and to real gingham. It should not replace the sage (see priority 9).

**Type.**
- **Fraunces** (Undercase Type: Phaedra Charles and Flavia Zimbardi; commissioned by Google Fonts in 2018) is modelled on early-20th-century "wonky" display faces such as Windsor, Souvenir and Cooper [24].
- Its designers trace that genre back to Arts and Crafts type, particularly **Morris's Golden Type** [25]. That lineage is our strongest type argument, and it is sourced.
- Fraunces has four axes: weight, opsz (9 to 144), SOFT (named states Sharp, Soft, SuperSoft; "wetness" or "inkiness") and WONK (swaps in leaning h, n and m and flagged italic terminals, automatically at 18pt and below) [24][25].
- **Lora** is credited to Cyreal and was added to Google Fonts in 2011, with a wght axis of 400 to 700 [26].
- **Caveat** is credited to Impallari Type and was added in 2015, also wght 400 to 700 [27].
- One of the researchers named individual designers for Lora and gave 2014 for Caveat. The Google Fonts metadata does not support either, so use the credits above.

**What is installed (package.json @fontsource) that could help.**
- **Caveat's weight axis (400 to 700)** can produce visibly different hands from one family. This costs nothing.
- **Pinyon Script** is a formal copperplate. At most it could be one "older hand" on a specimen label (tell 2). Keep it to a word or two, because it is hard to read at length.
- **IBM Plex Mono** could stand in for a typed card or an accession number. Schlesinger defines manuscript cookbooks as handwritten "or occasionally typed" [16]. Treat this as optional. The forbids list excludes only geometric sans display type.
- Stay in the current three families for everything else. No new commercial faces are needed.

## 6. Motion vocabulary

The founder wants purposeful transitions, possibly a distinct transition between each pair of schools, and loves how the wordmark moves while the page changes under it. These are ideas grounded in the sources. Anything original is labelled as such.

**Principle: specimens stay still. Hands and paper move.** A pressed flower is fixed, dry and flat [18]. Making specimens sway would contradict the school's central metaphor. The only things that move in this room are a hand (writing, taping, laying down paper) and light (the lamp and the fireflies). That rule also keeps the motion budget small, and restraint is itself a tell.

**Within-page motion (for Tier 3 or the transitions phase):**
1. **Write-on.** Handwritten kickers (`.cc-hand`) and the scribble underline (`.cc-scribble::after`) appear left to right once, as if being written. Use a clip-path inset, or animate the mask position. Grounded in handwriting as an added-later entry [17] and "the hand of man" [20].
2. **Tape laid.** A `.cc-tape` strip grows from one torn end (scaleX from its left or right origin) and settles to its tilt. Only then does the paper under it stop settling. The tape holds the paper, so it lands last [23].
3. **Press and settle.** Cards and sheets arrive slightly lifted (bigger shadow, about 1.01 scale) and settle flat onto their `--tilt`. No overshoot and no bounce, because pressing only goes one way [15][18]. Use compositor-only properties, as the theme.css comment on `cc-page-turn` already requires.
4. **Ink settling in Fraunces (original proposal, not documented practice).** Headings could land at SOFT 0 (Sharp) and ease to SOFT 100 (SuperSoft), like ink spreading into paper. SOFT is the "wetness" axis [24]. I found no example of anyone animating this axis, so present it to the founder as new. Test for glyph-width change and layout shift before adopting. WONK is a glyph swap, not a smooth axis, so it can only flip once and cannot tween. Never animate wght: it changes advance widths and reflows the text.
5. **Lift the flap.** Potter's 1890 toy picture hides an image under a flap [9]. That is a period-true reveal. Two candidates: the Sent page envelope flap (`Sent.astro .flap`) opening or closing, and a note that lifts to show the Lab list.
6. **Fireflies flash; they do not breathe.** Fireflies signal in flashes separated by darkness. The synchronous *Photinus carolinus* flashes five to eight times, then goes dark for about eight seconds, and it is one of only a few species that synchronise at all [28]. Use flash-then-dark bursts, each firefly on its own clock. Do not imitate the rare synchronised species. I could not open the peer-reviewed flash-timing paper one researcher cited, so tune exact intervals by eye.

**Arrival and transition ideas (for the later phase; all original):**
- **Laid on the table (entering Cottagecore).** The new page arrives as a sheet set down: a small translateY plus a collapsing shadow, then tapes apply, then kickers write on. Stagger all of it inside about 900 ms. Reduced motion jumps to the end state, which the current `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` block already does.
- **Pressed away (leaving Cottagecore).** The old snapshot flattens (scaleY slightly, shadow gone, opacity down) as if closed into the press. This matches the current `cc-set-down`, and should be made more deliberate.
- **The wordmark.** Keep `transition:name="wordmark"` as it is (the founder likes it). Once the group lands, the `.wm-hand` part could write itself on and the `.wm-daisy` could rotate into its -12deg rest, as if tucked in. Both happen after the shared-element move, never during it.
- **The current page-turn** (`cc-page-turn`, rotateY -16deg) is competent but generic, because any paper theme could use it. Pressing, taping and writing belong to this school alone.

## 7. Gap analysis against our build

I checked this against `theme.css`, `fx.ts`, `meta.ts`, `Header.astro`, `Footer.astro`, `assets/*`, all five pages, the mobile sheet `scripts/themes/.out/mobile-review/sheets/cottagecore.jpg`, and the desktop captures in `scripts/themes/.out/cottagecore-f3/`.

### 7.1 What already lands (protect)
- **Gingham follows real weave logic.** `theme.css .cc-gingham` crosses two 50% alpha thread gradients, so the crossings go darker in three tones, like a real dyed-yarn check [3]. The scalloped valance and hem read as cut cloth. Keep both.
- **Washi tape is translucent.** Each `--tape-*` is 0.78 to 0.82 alpha, with torn zigzag ends through clip-path [23]. Keep.
- **The specimens are good drawing.** `assets/Pressed.astro` draws named, recognisable species (daisy, lavender, fern, wild rose, birch twig with catkins) with firm edge strokes and seeded jitter, so no two presses match. That satisfies tells 1, 4 and 6 and Morris's firm outline [21]. The birch twig on About is held down with gum strips drawn into the SVG, which is correct mounting practice [18]. This is the build's best asset.
- **The About herbarium sheet.** Etymology sits in a double-ruled specimen label beside a mounted twig. It is the most canon-true composition in the school [1][18].
- **Recipe cards.** `№ 1` numbering, a rose double rule, and ruled lines under the text, like a recipe card [16].
- **The lamplight dark scheme.** It was designed, not inverted, and the Tier 1 doctrine already endorses it (`tier1-merged-findings.json`).
- **The fireflies already run on independent phases.** In `fx.ts` each mote gets its own random `phase` and a 3.2 to 7.4 s `period`, so they are not synchronised. One researcher assumed they ran on a shared timer; they do not. Keep the independence.
- **Fraunces with SOFT 100 and WONK 1** gives the soft, Arts-and-Crafts-descended display voice [24][25]. The two-part wordmark (a set name plus a handwritten remainder) reads as a jar label.
- **Mobile.** The handwritten nav with its stitched seam (`Header.astro`, 640px block) is a real phone design, not a fallback.

### 7.2 What is missing or wrong
1. **One hand, one ink, everywhere.** `.cc-hand`, `.wm-hand`, `Home .lead`, `.no`, `.f-loc`, the Contact field labels and the mobile nav all use Caveat at weight 600, mostly in `--accent` rose. The canon is several hands and inks added at different times [16][17], so today it reads as a font. This breaks tell 2.
2. **The Home herbarium label is a scribble placeholder.** `Home.astro .herb-label .hl-hand` draws wavy lines where writing should be. Real labels carry name, family, place, date and collector [18], and Dickinson labelled hers in her own hand [1]. The component comment admits the scribble "stands in for handwriting". This breaks tell 1, and it sits in the hero.
3. **The pressed specimens float.** `--press-shadow` is a drop-shadow, 0 1px 1px in light and 0 2px 3px in dark, on every `.pressed`. Pressed and mounted plants lie flat in the paper [15][18]. On the hero sheet it makes them read as stickers. This breaks tell 3. The flowers also stay at full fresh saturation. Pressed colour usually fades, but that is my judgement, not something the sources above say.
4. **The sampler is not stitched or signed.** In `About.astro`, the hoop sets its manifesto in Fraunces italic, with a small cross-stitch motif from `Sampler.astro`. A real sampler stitches its words and signs them with a name, age and year [14]. Keep the manifesto as live text for accessibility, and add a stitched maker's line charted in `Sampler.astro` (studio initials and a year only, never a person's name). This breaks tell 9.
5. **Nothing is dated or attributed anywhere.** Across the pages there are numbers but no dates, places or makers on any made object. This breaks tells 1 and 9. It pairs with items 1 and 2.
6. **Countable repeats.** `.cc-torn` repeats a single 240px edge tile, which is countable on the 44rem Lab note and the Services method sheet. Every tape uses the same clip-path. Morris would object [21]. This breaks tell 5.
7. **No collage.** The manuscripts mix handwriting with pasted printed clippings [16][17]. Everything in the build is either handwritten Caveat or clean Lora, with no pasted printed matter. The Lab specimens list on the Home butter note is the natural candidate for a clipped, printed slip.
8. **The light scheme has no motion of its own.** It has hover rotation on `.cc-label`, the arrow nudge on `.cc-note-link`, and the generic `cc-page-turn`. Nothing on arrival says "hand at work" (section 6, items 1 to 3). Dark mode has the fireflies, so the light scheme, which is the native one, is the less alive of the two.
9. **The fireflies breathe instead of flashing.** The `fx.ts` pulse is `pow(0.5 + 0.5 sin, 3)`: a smooth continuous glow. Real signalling is flash then dark [28]. It needs a small change to the pulse function, and the independence should stay.
10. **The Fraunces axes carry no hierarchy.** Every h1 to h3, `.pull`, `.closer-lead`, `.wm-name` and `.f-name` is set to `'SOFT' 100, 'WONK' 1`. The billboard, card titles and pull quotes all read the same wetness. Varying SOFT by role (SuperSoft billboard, Soft card heads) would widen the range, and it sets up the animated settle in section 6.

## 8. Priorities for Tier 3

In order, most important first:

1. **Give the page several hands.** Define two or three handwriting voices from Caveat's 400 to 700 weight axis, with inks in rose-deep, sepia (the existing ink-brown family) and sage, and small differences in size and tilt. Assign them by role: kicker, margin note, label. Add a new `meta.contrast` entry for each new ink against `--paper` and `--note`. Sources [16][17]; files `theme.css .cc-hand`, `Header.astro`, pages.
2. **Replace placeholders with real labels.** Write a real specimen label on the Home hero sheet (name, place, a date, a collector line in the studio's voice), and consider small labels on key specimens. The copy is the founder's to decide, so draft it and flag it. Dates follow the founder's MM-DD-YY rule unless a period style is chosen deliberately. Sources [1][18]; file `Home.astro .herb-label`.
3. **Flatten the pressed specimens.** Reduce `--press-shadow` to a hairline contact shadow or remove it, so they read as mounted rather than stuck on. Sources [15][18]; file `theme.css`.
4. **Arrival choreography within the page.** Write-on for kickers and scribbles, tape laid, then paper pressed and settled. Compositor-only, one pass, with the reduced-motion end state. This is the groundwork for the transitions phase. Sources [17][18][23]; files `theme.css`, a small scroll-in observer next to `fx.ts`.
5. **Firefly flash rhythm.** Change the pulse to a short flash followed by a dark interval, keeping each firefly on its own clock. Source [28]; file `fx.ts draw()`.
6. **Hide the repeats.** Add two or three torn-edge tile variants, or one much longer tile, and a few tape clip-path variants chosen per instance. Source [21]; file `theme.css .cc-torn` and `.cc-tape`.
7. **Stitch and sign the sampler.** Chart a studio-initials-and-year line in `Sampler.astro`, placed below the motif. Source [14].
8. **One pasted clipping.** Set the Lab specimen list as a printed slip pasted onto the butter note: a different paper tone, straight trimmed edges, Lora small caps or Plex Mono. Sources [16][17]; file `Home.astro .specimens`.
9. **Minor, optional.** Vary SOFT by heading role, and trial the SOFT settle as an original proposal to take to the founder [24]. Try one indigo thread (a tape or an ink) to tie the build to Morris and to real gingham [2][3], without replacing the sage.
