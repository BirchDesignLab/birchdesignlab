## 1. The school in one paragraph

The Bauhaus was a state school of art and craft founded by Walter Gropius in Weimar on 04-12-1919, moved to Dessau in 1925, and closed in Berlin in 1933 [24]. Its founding programme set building as the goal of all art and called the artist "an exalted artisan" [26]. In Dessau Gropius replaced the craft emphasis with "art and technology, a new unity" [24], and the school became a workshop for industry: loose-leaf product catalogues printed in black and red with plain photographs and no designer credits [4], typefaces built from geometry instead of handwriting [12][16], and a stage where bodies were costumed into geometry and choreographed along verticals and diagonals [19][20]. An originator would recognise: construction from a few elementary forms, the workshop and the product, black and red print on white, lowercase grotesque type after 1925, photography used as fact, and the red square, yellow triangle and blue circle as one teacher's theory put to work [7][23]. They would not recognise "form follows function" as their slogan (trap 1), nor a page of primary-colour confetti with no structural reason for any shape.

## 3. Defining tells (critic's checklist)

Apply to each page. Each item is a test a critic can pass or fail.

1. **Construction from a few primitives.** Can every graphic element be named as a circle, square, triangle, bar or rule? Bayer's alphabet and Albers's type are built this way [1][12][16]; so is Schmidt's poster [6] and Kandinsky's theory [25].
2. **The pairing is exact where it is claimed.** Yellow triangle, red square, blue circle [7][23]. Black and white are outside the triad. A red circle or a yellow square is a different system.
3. **One case, and it is lower.** Dessau-period type is single-case: the universal alphabet drops the case distinction [12], and the late printshop sets the modern in lowercase grotesque [17]. Letterspaced capitals belong to Weimar 1923 [8] or to later schools, not to a lowercase house.
4. **Black and red is the working ink.** The product catalogue is black and red on white [3][4]; the full triad is the poster and the theory [6][7]. A page that is all poster and no printshop misses half the school.
5. **Rules and circles do typographic work.** Heavy left rules set off passages; bold circles break sections [11]. A rule on our pages should separate or indent something, not decorate.
6. **The diagonal exists.** Schmidt's cross is on the diagonal [6]; the stage choreography stresses verticals and diagonals [20]. An all-orthogonal page drifts toward De Stijl (trap 2).
7. **Photography as fact.** Objective product photographs [4]; typophoto, type and photograph as one message [11][17]. Illustration is not the only image register.
8. **Modules lock on a grid.** Interconnecting cubes [27]; Schlemmer's ruled grid of figures [2]; Schmidt's nine-field exercises [18]. Nothing floats.
9. **Contrast of size as the drama.** Schmidt's course contrasted identical elements by size, position and colour [18]. Big against small, not many things at medium size.
10. **Anonymous, product-first voice.** Products carried code numbers, not designer names [4]. Credits, signatures and personality flourishes are not the register.

## 4. Traps

1. **"Form follows function" is not a Bauhaus slogan.** It is Louis Sullivan's, in "The Tall Office Building Artistically Considered", *Lippincott's*, 1896, where it appears as "form ever follows function" [28]. The Bauhaus's own Dessau motto was "art and technology, a new unity" [24]. Our `meta.ts` lesson line opens with the Sullivan phrase.
2. **De Stijl read as Bauhaus.** The black-ruled orthogonal grid with primary rectangles is the Dutch neighbour. Van Doesburg and Mondrian were published *by* the Bauhaus in its book series [9], which is the connection, not the identity. The Bauhaus canon keeps the circle and the diagonal [6][7].
3. **New Typography read as Bauhaus doctrine.** Tschichold's *Die neue Typographie* (1928) is an anthology of the wider international movement; Tschichold and others were "not directly affiliated with the Bauhaus" but in dialogue with it [13]. Never write "the Bauhaus said" over a Tschichold idea.
4. **Futura read as the Bauhaus face.** Paul Renner was not Bauhaus faculty, and Futura appeared in only one or two late Bauhaus productions [14]. It is a contemporary geometric sans, fair to use as a period neighbour, not as "the Bauhaus typeface".
5. **ITC Bauhaus and the phototype revival.** The rounded "Bauhaus" display face descends from 1960s and 1970s experimental alphabets that took up Bayer's and Albers's ideas [12]. Rounded, soft geometric display type reads as that revival.
6. **Primary-colour confetti.** Shapes scattered without a structural job, in any colour on any shape, is the stock-graphic cliché. The canon's shapes are assembled into one composition [6][27] and follow a declared pairing [7].
7. **Treating the Kandinsky pairing as universal Bauhaus law.** It was one teacher's theory; the questionnaires were first developed for INKhUK students in Moscow and then used at the Bauhaus [23]. The build may adopt it as a house rule (recommended), but wall-label copy should credit Kandinsky, not "the Bauhaus".
8. **Weimar capitals and Dessau lowercase in one voice.** The 1923 catalogue used capitals [8]; after 1925 the school went lowercase [12][17]. Mixing both in one page is an era collision.

## 5. Palette and type evidence

**Palette.** No source consulted gives colour values. What the sources establish is structure:
- The theory triad: yellow, red, blue, each on its own shape [7][23].
- The printshop pair: black and red on white [3][4].
- Stage grounds: yellow, pink and black series [19]; costumes in primary and metallic colour on a cream sheet [2].

Build values (`src/themes/bauhaus/theme.css`): paper #f3efe6, ink #141414, red #d42a1e, yellow #f4c214, blue #1e4aa8 (dark: #3a57d8, #f7c61a on #121212). These are the build's choices, sensible for the canon; do not cite them as "Bauhaus colours". Measured contrasts, for the revisers:

Red as a second ink is exactly what the printshop did [3][4]. Whether to loosen the doctrine is the founder's call (priority 6).

**Type.** What the sources show: single-case geometric construction as the ideal [1][12]; lowercase grotesque in real Dessau print [17]; capitals in Weimar 1923 [8]; Futura as a contemporary neighbour, not the school's face [14]. ## 6. Motion vocabulary

Grounded in the sources, for the in-page pass and the later transitions phase.

**Within the page**
- **Assembly is canonical; the spring is not.** Keler's cradle, Schmidt's poster and the Dessau building all present separate primary forms locked into one composition [6][7][27], so "assembled on load" is well founded. Schlemmer's principle is that the costume, not the dancer's mobility, determines the movement [22]; the stage stressed verticals and diagonals [20]. That argues for travel along one axis or one diagonal, rotation about a fixed pivot, and a hard landing, over a bouncy overshoot. The build's `--ease-land` (`cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.9, 0.25, 1.12)`) overshoots by 12 percent; consider a no-overshoot land, or a single mechanical stop, for the assembly.
- **Three series, not one curve.** The Triadic Ballet runs as three colour series: yellow, then pink, then black [19], with moods the Schlemmer estate describes as serene-burlesque, ceremonious and mystical-fantastic [22]. A page's shapes could arrive in three discrete beats (triangle, square, circle) with distinct tempos rather than one staggered cascade.
- **Tempo.** Moholy-Nagy's typophoto chapter is paced like film [11]. Scroll reveals can vary pace section by section: fast for a numbered list, slow for the manifesto slab.
- **Motor, not muscle.** The Light Prop is a motor-driven rotation of perforated and reflective parts through a light beam [5]. A slow continuous rotation of one element (a perforated disc behind the hero, a ring) fits; no source gives its speed, so do not attribute any duration to Moholy-Nagy.
- **Size contrast as motion.** Schmidt's exercise changed one element's size and position to change its reading [18]. A shape that scales from small to dominant on hover or scroll is on-canon.

**Arrival and transition ideas (later phase)**
- **The trio as masks.** The header mark is circle, square, triangle. A cross-school arrival can open through each in turn (triangle, then square, then circle) as a three-beat wipe, the Triadic Ballet structure applied to navigation [19].
- **The diagonal cross.** Bauhaus-to-Bauhaus navigation as a wipe along a 45 degree axis, after Schmidt's poster [6], replacing the current left-to-right square wipe.
- **Disassembly on leave.** The page's shapes fly off along their axes before the next page assembles: construction visibly reversed, then redone.
- **The rotating disc.** A perforated disc mask rotating once as the new page shows through its holes, after the Light Prop [5] and *Lightplay* [5]. Heavy; a candidate for the crown-jewel treatment the founder described.
- **The wordmark as a machine part.** The morphing wordmark the founder likes can travel with its three shapes separating and re-docking, each on its own axis, rather than moving as one block.
- **Reduced motion.** The finished poster, as now.

## 7. Gap analysis against our build

**What already lands (protect it)**
- `Header.astro`: the trio mark (blue circle, red square, yellow triangle) follows the pairing exactly [7]; the nav's current page as a solid red block is a printshop move; the heavy closing rule is structural [11].
- `pages/Home.astro` hero: stacked lowercase name with the staircase step, beside a poster composition whose primary shapes follow the pairing and lock onto a bar [6][27]. This is the page the founder rates; keep its structure.
- The twelve-column grid and heavy rules throughout (`theme.css` `.grid12`, `--rule: 6px`); `Services.astro` offering body hangs from a heavy left rule, which is Moholy-Nagy's device almost exactly [11].
- Primaries as blocks (`.block-red`, `.block-yellow`, `.block-blue`) with ink or paper text on them; numbered modules; the vertical label on the lab band.
- Assembly on load and reveal gating (`.asm`, `data-reveal`), with reduced motion getting the finished poster.
- Dark scheme as the poster on a black wall has a precedent in the Triadic Ballet's black series [19].
- `pages/Sent.astro`: yellow triangle, red square, blue circle all correct, and the circle rolling into the square is a small story told in primitives.

**Missing or wrong**
1. **The pairing claim in `theme.css` is false in places.** The comment says the shapes on every page keep Kandinsky's pairing [7]. Violations:
   - `Footer.astro` `.corner`: red circle and yellow square. On every page, and the last shape a visitor sees.
   - `pages/Home.astro` `.lab-shapes`: yellow circle on the red band.
   - `pages/Contact.astro` `.talk`: red circle beside the blue one.
   - `pages/About.astro` `.letter-b`: loose yellow circle; `.tree`: yellow circle; the B's red bowl.
   - `pages/Home.astro` hero ring in dark: `--poster-mark` becomes red, so a red circle appears.
   Neutral (ink, paper) shapes are outside the triad and fine anywhere.
2. **Letterspaced capitals throughout a lowercase house** (tell 3, trap 8). `.kicker`, `.btn`, `.more` in `theme.css`; `.loc` in `Footer.astro`; `label` in `pages/Contact.astro`; the vertical lab kicker. Headings and nav are lowercase ([17]; the header comment says so), then every label shouts in spaced capitals, which reads Swiss or corporate and collides with the lowercase doctrine.
3. **Unbounded numerals** (`--font-num`) have no period basis. The big 01/02 numerals on Home doors, Services offerings and steps are the most repeated ornament on the site and the best place for constructed type [1][16].
4. **No diagonal anywhere** (tell 6). Every composition is orthogonal except the triangles' own edges. Schmidt's diagonal cross [6] and the stage's diagonals [20] are missing.
5. **No printshop register** (tell 4). Every page is the full-triad poster. Nothing uses the black-and-red working ink of the catalogue [3][4], so the site shows the theory but not the workshop.
6. **No photography** (tell 7). Typophoto [11] and objective product photography [4] are absent. The lab band's specimen list on Home is the natural place: lab case studies already carry a `hero` image in the schema (`src/lib/lab-schema.ts`). A grayscale or halftone crop between heavy rules would be typophoto.
7. **Overshooting assembly ease** (`--ease-land`) reads as spring rather than mechanism (section 6). Minor, and the founder likes the motion; test before changing.
8. **`meta.ts` lesson line** opens with "Form follows function", which is Sullivan's [28] (trap 1).
9. **Capture check, not necessarily a bug.** In the desktop capture `bauhaus-f3/t-bauhaus__light__desktop.png` the Home lab-band shapes and the closer see-saw show only their static parts (the see-saw base rule), while the mobile sheet shows them assembled. Likely the reveal gate parking off-screen compositions during a full-page capture; confirm in a real scroll before shipping.

## 8. Priorities for Tier 3

Most important first.

1. **Make the pairing true everywhere, then keep the comment.** Primary shapes obey yellow triangle, red square, blue circle; ink and paper are free. Fixes:
   - Footer corner: yellow triangle and red square on the ink band. Blue on ink is 2.28, too dim.
   - Home lab band: yellow triangle plus ink circle, or a paper circle.
   - Contact: blue circle meeting a red square, a conversation between two shapes.
   - About: yellow triangle in place of the loose circle; the tree's sun as a paper circle.
   - Dark `--poster-mark`: paper, not red.
   The B's red bowl is a judgement call. It reads as a constructed letter, but flag it to the founder. [7][23]
2. **Go lowercase everywhere.** Kickers, buttons, "more" links, form labels and the footer location in lowercase, carrying hierarchy by weight, size, rule and the leading shape instead of tracking and capitals. Keep a little tracking if it helps small labels, but no `text-transform: uppercase`. [12][17]
3. **Construct the numerals.** Replace Unbounded 01/02 (Home doors, Services offerings, process steps) with SVG numerals built from bars and circles on a small module, assembled with the existing `.asm` machinery. This becomes the school's signature detail. Move the small designations to League Spartan. [1][16]
4. **Add one diagonal composition per page type.** One module set on a 45 degree axis, for example Services intro or the Sent composition as a diagonal cross, after Schmidt [6][20]. One per page; more turns into confetti.
5. **Introduce the printshop register on the working pages.** Contact form and Services bodies in black with red as the second ink (red rules, red leading squares, one red display word), keeping full-triad blocks for the poster moments. Pairs with priority 6. [3][4]
6. **Founder decision: red as large-text ink.** Red on paper passes 3:1 for large text (4.41); a darker red (#b8231a, 5.56) passes at any size. Loosening "primaries never text" to "red may be ink at display sizes" is period-true [3][4] but reverses a declared doctrine, so it is not the revisers' call alone.
7. **Typophoto in the lab band.** Grayscale case-study hero crops between heavy rules beside each designation, using existing `hero` images where a specimen has one. [4][11]
8. **Mechanical assembly.** Trial a no-overshoot `--ease-land` and three-beat arrival (triangle, square, circle) on the hero. Keep whichever the founder prefers. [19][20][22]
9. **Fix the lesson line.** Replace "Form follows function" with the school's own motto, "art and technology, a new unity" [24]. Credit Kandinsky by name in any wall-label mention of the colour pairing [23].

## 9. Dropped, corrected or unverified

- **Kandinsky questionnaire scholarship.** The two reports named different authors for the Project MUSE article (Fallon and Bermeitinger vs Behrens and Zimmer). MUSE served a bot challenge, so I could not resolve it. Dropped. The claim that Klee and Schlemmer preferred other pairings rested only on that article: dropped. No image or transcript of the original questionnaire was found.
- **Triadic Ballet premiere.** One report gave 09-30-1922 with music by Hindemith. The Schlemmer estate gives 09-22-1922 and says Hindemith scored the 1926 performance [22]. The Bauhaus-Archiv record confirms only a 1922 Stuttgart premiere [19]. Use "Stuttgart, 1922".
- **Albers's construction.** Letterform Archive lists square, triangle and quarter circle; the Bauhaus institutions' record lists circle, quarter circle and square [16]. The institutional record is used.
- **Bayer sheet details.** "Compass-point indents" and "collaged corrections" on BR48.94 were not in the museum text I read; dropped. The inscriptions are confirmed [1].
- **Punkt und Linie cover by Bayer.** Unverified. The institutions' Bauhausbücher page credits Moholy-Nagy with most covers and names Bayer for volume 13 [9].
- **Schlemmer's 1922 signet.** The only image source found is a modern vector recreation (People's Graphic Design Archive). Its existence and use on Schmidt's poster are confirmed [6]; its exact drawing is not. The bauhauskooperation "signet" page describes Karl Peter Röhl's 1919 star-manikin, a different mark.
- **MoMA records** for Schmidt's poster (lithograph, dimensions), Breuer's B3 chair, Bayer's Kandinsky birthday poster and the 1923 catalogue returned 403. Not cited. Schmidt's poster is cited from the Bauhaus-Archiv record [6] instead.
- **MoMA 2009 exhibition framing** (Bergdoll and Dickerman, "workshops for modernity"): page returned 403. Dropped.
- **Dessau building details** (curtain wall, pinwheel plan): the Stiftung page had no body text and the architecture writeups were weak. Replaced with UNESCO's own text [27].
- **Light Prop timing and film running time**: no source gives a rotation speed or a single running time. None stated.
- **Kandinsky on the diagonal** (warm vertical, cold horizontal): not in the publisher text I read [25]. Dropped.
- **Bayer's letterhead line about capitals**: the only source (a Print magazine article) returned 403, and no museum record of the letterhead was reachable. The lowercase practice is supported by [12] and [17]; the quote is dropped.
