# Stage 6, stop 1: the set re-review (for the founder)

Drafted 11-19-26 by the set critic (Opus `high`) from the six school
reports, the glass drag diagnosis, the ledger triage, the boundary sheets
and the Stage 0 set brief. The per-school reports in this folder hold the
detail and the code references; this page holds the calls.

## Where the set stands

The set still works and the three close pairs still read apart: I looked at
every boundary sheet in dark and on phones, and nobody would confuse two
rooms at thumbnail size. Your ranking still matches the captures. Vaporwave
and bauhaus set the bar; grandmillennial and cottagecore sell; swiss reads
as "a normal, tidy website" because it has one hidden toy and a half-empty
desktop hero; glass is the weakest, for two separate reasons. First, this
machine has Windows "Transparency effects" off, so the browser asks every
site for less transparency and glass shows its opaque fallback with the lens
hidden: if you looked on this machine, that alone is what you saw (check
Settings > Personalization > Colors > Transparency effects). Second, even with transparency on, the
wallpaper is already a blur, so frosting it changes nothing, and the lens
gets lost under the panes after a drag. Both laggards can close most of the
gap in this stage. Three things are shared across the reports: Stage 0's
rule that every school re-expresses its process row (S8) was applied in
only three of six schools; toys are hard to find or missing below the first
screen in several schools; and tablet (820) is the weakest width in most reports.
The honest scale is bigger than the plan: everything proposed is about
three waves, not one (see "Proposed wave").

## Calls for you

1. **Glass: what to do when a visitor's computer asks for less
   transparency.**
   (a) Ignore the setting in the Portal and always show real glass, keeping
   only the high-contrast setting. This matches your Stage 0 ruling on
   reduced motion ("a lab thing and a portfolio piece").
   (b) Honour it with a "Mica" fallback: wallpaper, orbs and lens stay, and
   panes go opaque but tinted from the wallpaper under them, as Windows 11
   does. This is the glass critic's pick.
   (c) Keep today's flat fallback.
   **Recommend (a).** A glass room that turns its glass off for anyone with
   the Windows toggle off is exactly the failure you reported, and your
   reduced-motion ruling already says the Lab does not tone itself down.
   (b) is a fair second choice if you would rather be polite to the setting.

2. **Glass rework: R1 alone, or R1 then R2.**
   R1 (moderate): repaint the wallpapers with crisp layered waves in amber,
   coral and deep blue (no mauve), so the frost has something to blur; thin
   the glass to something clear, with a visible bent rim and a highlight that
   follows the pointer on every pane; a clearer header.
   R2 (large): the lens becomes Liquid Glass over the page, bending real
   text and controls; header and footer become clear lensing bars; two or
   three more glass toys. Full effect in Chrome and Edge only; Safari (iPhone
   included) and Firefox keep today's lens.
   **Recommend R1 in this wave, and an R2 proof (a Home-only sheet of the new
   lens bending real text) run alongside it and shown at stop 2. If you like
   it, R2 becomes its own stage rather than squeezing into this one.** R2 is
   the Liquid Glass direction you set in Stage 0, so it deserves its own look
   before it is built.
   Sub-call: R1 repaints all six wallpapers, the one taste risk in the glass
   work. Either (a) go straight in and judge it at stop 2 (reversible), or
   (b) a short wallpaper proof first, adding a quick look. **Recommend (a).**

3. **Swiss's big pick.**
   (a) P4, "typophoto": a hard black-and-white or red duotone photograph band
   under the Home billboard, cropped by the page edge, and the same kind of
   image replacing About's tree (you said you would rather a different
   image). Needs one photograph, no people, which you supply or approve.
   (b) P3, re-division: a key that cycles the page's grid between 2, 3, 4 and
   6 columns and re-lays the page live. The Portal's idea inside one room and
   the highest identity move, but heavy to build and test.
   (c) Neither.
   **Recommend (a) in this wave, if you can supply or approve a photograph,
   and P3 as a Home-only proof shown at stop 2, built later as its own stage
   if you like it.** Without (a), About keeps the tree for now, with the
   manifesto and button moved down to the foot of the red field.
   Also: **P1** (the grid key becomes a full mark-up layer: red column
   numbers, span tags, the giant's type spec, and a tap that latches),
   **P2** (one variable-type toy per page: Home the width dial, About the
   rotated "Birch" widening as you scroll, Services a numeral you hold to
   change weight, Sent the red full stop you can drag), **P5** (a bigger
   desktop billboard so the top-right is no longer empty). **Recommend all
   three.** P5 goes in before the width dial.

## Proposed wave

Everything above is about 20 tasks, roughly 220 to 260 agents, against the
plan's 4 to 6 tasks and 60 to 90. Too big for one wave. Recommend three
waves in this stage, each ending at a stop, with two proofs alongside the
first. Runtime tasks (WebGL, the lens, the portal lifecycle) run their
implementer and fixer at Opus `medium`.

**Wave 1, the laggards and the two parked toys (stop 2)**, about 85 to 100
agents:
1. Glass: the two drag bugs, the reduce-mode policy (call 1). Runtime. 10.
2. Glass R1, part 1: the repainted wallpapers, the palette, the thinner
   material. Runtime (the lens texture follows the wallpaper). 15.
3. Glass R1, part 2: the bent rim, the pointer highlight, the header, the
   tablet Control Centre and the minors. Runtime. 15.
4. Swiss: P5, flush-left, the width dial with its overflow guard, P1 and
   the latching grid key, the tablet key, the dark lab band (call 9). 15.
5. Swiss: P4 if taken (waits on the photograph), the timetable process,
   phone numerals, About. 12.
6. Bauhaus: the questionnaire card. 12.
- Alongside: the R2 lens proof (runtime, about 5 agents) and the P3 Home
  proof (about 5), both shown at stop 2.

**Wave 2, the four strong schools and S8 (stop 3)**, about 75 to 90 agents:
7. Bauhaus: the process diagram, the unequal boxes, the findable shapes,
   the footer stripe, the phone swipe. 12.
8. Vaporwave: V1 to V11, plus V12 and V14 if taken. 15.
9. Grandmillennial: rosette tails, the ribbon strip, the frames task
   (half roses, centred specimen), the cameo swag, the trellis intros,
   GM5 and GM6. 15.
10. Grandmillennial: the ledger leftovers. 10 to 12.
11. Cottagecore: fireflies and the tap (canvas work), seeds, hovers, CC5,
    CC6. 12. Treat as runtime if the canvas changes grow.
12. Set: the hover guard test and any stragglers. 10.

**Wave 3, the new interactables (stop 4)**, about 60 to 75 agents, from
call 7: vaporwave (the VCR remote touches the sunset's WebGL grid, so
runtime), grandmillennial, cottagecore, bauhaus, swiss P2 and P6, then the
three big ones. About 5 to 6 tasks.

Then the close as planned (merge main, gates, report, PR). If three waves
is more than you want before transitions, the cleanest trim is to move wave
3 to its own stage after transitions; waves 1 and 2 are what even the set
out.
