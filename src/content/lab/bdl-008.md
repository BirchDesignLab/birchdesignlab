Most salon sites on this coast are a business card with a phone number on
it: nice photos, "call to book," and no prices anywhere. A salon sells time
and money. Both belong on the page, where somebody can settle the question
at eleven at night without calling anyone.

Magnolia & Mane is a salon we made up, in Ocean Springs, with a real site
built for it. There is no client. It exists so the argument has something
you can click.

## The prices are on the page

Signature Cut & Style, $65. Gel Manicure, $45. Classic Pedicure, $55. Color
is "from $120," and the page says why: it depends on length and density, and
the stylist confirms the number before any color goes on. A range you read
at home is candor. The same range discovered in the chair is a bait and
switch.

## Booking stays on the site

On most salon sites, Book Now is where the site gives up and hands you to
somebody else's domain. Here the calendar opens on the salon's own page,
under its own header. It is a booking provider's calendar, not one we wrote,
and it does not load until you ask for it.

## Two languages

English at the root, Vietnamese under `/vi/`, each a real page with its own
address. You change language by clicking; nothing guesses for you. The
services, the questions and the reviews are translated. Only the legal
pages stay in English.

The Vietnamese here is ours, and no native speaker has read it. On a
client's site one would, before any customer did.

## What it leaves out

No ad pixels, no trackers, no third-party scripts on the homepage. Visits
are counted by a counter on the site's own domain, and it does not know who
you are.

## The same floor as everything else

A salon takes no orders and no payments. It is held to the same standard as
a site that does: every page has to score 95 or better for speed,
accessibility and best practices, or the build fails.
